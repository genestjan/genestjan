What we help with

If any of this sounds like your household, an assessment is the right next step.

◆

Sensory processing

Meltdowns at the supermarket, clothing tags, food textures, noise. Not fussiness — a nervous system asking for help.

◆

Fine motor & handwriting

Pencil grip, scissors, buttons, shoelaces. The everyday skills that quietly shape how a child feels at school.

◆

Daily routines

Getting dressed, mealtimes, bedtime, transitions. When ordinary parts of the day take an extraordinary amount of effort.

What your first visit looks like

1

We listen first

You talk, we take notes. You've been carrying observations for months — we want all of them.

2

We assess through play

Your child plays. We watch how they move, respond and problem-solve. No tests that feel like tests.

3

You leave with a plan

A written report and clear next steps in plain language — not clinical jargon you have to decode later.

Common questions

Do I need a referral?

No. You can book an assessment directly. If you're claiming through a funding scheme we'll tell you exactly what paperwork you need.

My child is very shy. Will they cope?

Yes. The first session is play-based and led at their pace. You stay in the room the whole time.

What if the assessment finds nothing?

Then you get peace of mind in writing, plus practical strategies for what you've been noticing. That's a good outcome too.
