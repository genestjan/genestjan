# 03. Coaching playbook

The football, psychology and safety content the app runs on. The Builder turns each section into validated data files under `src/content/`. **Coach James (and the programme leads) review this document before it goes into the app**; their changes win. Everything here is standard grassroots coaching practice written in plain words, with sources in `01-research-and-benchmark.md`.

## 1. Age bands and match formats

Philippine practice differs from the US model in the first plan: the PFF treats **8v8 as the standard for 13 and under** and runs futsal alongside it. Formats below are defaults per band; every match can override them.

| Band | Typical ages | Default match format | Also used | Main focus | Training |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **U8** | 6 to 8 | 4v4 no keepers, or 5v5 with keepers **[confirm]** | Futsal 4v4 + GK, festivals | Lots of touches, dribbling, being brave on the ball, fun, balance and coordination | 45 to 60 min, 1 to 2 a week |
| **U10** | 8 to 10 | 7v7 or 8v8 **[confirm]** | Futsal 4v4 + GK | First touch, passing to a teammate, 1v1 moves, first ideas of attack and defence | 60 to 75 min, 2 a week |
| **U12** | 10 to 12 | **8v8** (PFF U11 standard) | Futsal | Skills under pressure, 1v1 and 2v1, basic team shape, every child tries every position | 75 min, 2 to 3 a week |
| **U14** | 12 to 14 | **8v8 at U13**, 11v11 from U14 **[confirm]** | Futsal | Positions and units, combination play, aerobic base, bodyweight strength | 75 to 90 min, 3 a week |
| **U16** | 14 to 16 | 11v11 | Futsal | Team shape, formations, pressing and transitions, speed and strength | 90 min, 3 a week |

Rules for every band:
- **Development over results.** No standings for U10 and below inside the app.
- **Everyone plays.** Fair minutes are the default in all non-final matches; the head coach can switch a tournament knockout to "competitive" mode, which still guarantees every available child at least 40% of the match **[confirm]**.
- **Positions rotate until U12.** Every outfield position, and goal for anyone willing.
- **Short instructions, long playing time.** Aim for at least 70% of session time with a ball at the feet.
- Session times are guidance for development clubs; once or twice a week is fine for grassroots.

## 2. Coaching principles every screen should reflect

1. Ball for every child where possible; no lines longer than three children.
2. Play first, coach in short bursts (under 30 seconds), then play again.
3. Ask before telling: "What did you see?" "Where was the space?"
4. Praise effort and specific actions, by name, often. Aim for about five encouraging comments for every correction (a Positive Coaching Alliance guideline, not a strict rule).
5. Make it easier or harder with **STEP**: change the **S**pace, the **T**ask, the **E**quipment or the **P**eople (numbers).
6. Every child leaves having touched the ball hundreds of times and having heard their name said positively.

## 3. Session template

Every session has the same six slots so the builder only fills them. Times change by band.

| Slot | What happens | U8 (60) | U10 (75) | U12 (80) | U14 (90) | U16 (90) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. **Arrival play** | Small games start as kids arrive; no waiting | 10 | 10 | 8 | 5 | 0 |
| 2. **Warm-up** | FIFA 11+ Kids under 14; FIFA 11+ from 14 | 15 | 15 | 15 | 20 | 20 |
| 3. **Skill block** | Ball mastery or one technique, every child with a ball | 10 | 12 | 12 | 15 | 15 |
| 4. **Game-like practice** | Small games that repeat the skill against opponents (2v1, 3v2, 4v4) | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 | 25 |
| 5. **Play** | Free game; for the youngest this is the biggest part | 12 | 20 | 22 | 20 | 25 |
| 6. **Huddle** | Two questions, recognition, next week's challenge | 3 | 3 | 3 | 5 | 5 |

Water breaks are added automatically from the heat check (section 9.1).

**The huddle script** (shown in run mode):
1. "What was fun today?"
2. "What did you try that was new?"
3. Recognise one or more children for a club value ("Ana, Perseverance: you kept chasing back").
4. Next week's challenge ("Show me 20 sole rolls with your weaker foot").

### Drill write-ups (the format every drill follows)

Each drill record has: **Setup**, **How to play**, **Three coaching points**, **Easier** (STEP), **Harder** (STEP), **Diagram** (board steps), **Video** (optional YouTube link, approved by a coach). Two examples; the Builder drafts the rest in the same format and a coach approves each one before it is marked "reviewed".

**D04 Sharks and Minnows (6 to 10)**
- Setup: 20 x 15 m box. Most kids (minnows) have a ball at one end. Two sharks, no ball, in the middle.
- How to play: minnows dribble to the other end without losing the ball. Sharks kick balls out of the box. A minnow who loses their ball becomes a shark. Last minnow wins; play again.
- Coaching points: keep the ball close when a shark is near; look up for space; change speed to escape.
- Easier: wider box, one shark only, sharks walk.
- Harder: smaller box, minnows must use their weaker foot, minnows score a point by stopping the ball on the end line with the sole.

**D19 Pressure and Cover 2v2 (11+)**
- Setup: 25 x 20 m, a mini-goal at each end. 2v2 with a queue of pairs behind each goal.
- How to play: attackers try to score; when the ball goes out, the next pair comes on.
- Coaching points: first defender presses the ball quickly and stays side-on; second defender covers at an angle about 3 to 5 m behind; talk ("I've got the ball", "Cover left").
- Easier: attackers may only pass back once; bigger goals for defenders to protect less.
- Harder: 3v3 with a balance player; attackers get a bonus for scoring within 6 seconds.

### Equipment-light tips (shown in the session builder)
- No bibs: two teams in shirts inside out, or one team with shirts tucked in.
- Not enough balls: pairs share one ball and take turns as attacker and defender; skill blocks in "one ball per two" mode.
- No cones: slippers, water bottles, shoes, bottle caps.
- No goals: two cones 2 m apart; or score by stopping the ball on a line.
- Check ball pressure before each session; flat balls teach bad habits.

## 6. Strategies (presets for the board)

Each preset has steps for the animated board, a "Why it works" line written for kids, and a common mistake to warn about. The app only offers presets that suit the team's band and format.

### 6.1 Team shapes

| Shape | Format | Ages | What it looks like | Why it works (for kids) | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Diamond 1-2-1 | 4v4, 5v5 (with GK) | 6+ | One at the back, two wide, one up front | You always have two friends to pass to, in a triangle | All four chasing the ball in a bunch |
| Futsal 2-2 (box) | Futsal | 8+ | Two back, two front | Simple roles; hard to get past | Front two forgetting to help defend |
| Futsal 3-1 | Futsal | 11+ | Three in a line, one pivot up front | The pivot holds the ball while others join | Pivot turning into trouble instead of laying it off |
| 2-3-1 | 7v7 | 8 to 10 | Two defenders, three midfielders, one striker | Safe at the back, enough in the middle to keep the ball | Defenders never joining the attack |
| **3-3-1** | **8v8** | 9 to 13 | Three defenders, three midfielders, one striker | The middle three link everything; wide players make the pitch big | Striker left alone with no one close enough to pass |
| **2-3-2** | **8v8** | 10 to 13 | Two defenders, three midfielders, two strikers | Two strikers can press their defenders together | A gap behind the two defenders |
| 3-2-3 | 9v9 | 11 to 12 | Three defenders, two central midfielders, two wingers, one striker | Wingers stretch the other team and make gaps in the middle | Wingers drifting inside and losing the width |
| 4-3-3 | 11v11 | 13+ | Back four, three midfielders, two wingers, one striker | Lots of triangles; wingers attack 1v1 | Midfield three standing in a flat line |
| 4-4-2 | 11v11 | 13+ | Two lines of four, two strikers | Compact and easy to learn | Gap between the two lines of four |
| 4-2-3-1 | 11v11 | 15+ | Back four, two holding midfielders, three attacking midfielders, one striker | Two holders protect the defence so the front four can attack | Number 10 hiding instead of showing for the ball |

### 6.2 Moves and situations

| Move | Ages | What happens | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| Give-and-go (wall pass) | 7+ | Pass to a teammate, run past the defender, get it back | The defender can only watch the ball or the runner, not both |
| Stay goal-side | 7+ | Defenders stay between the attacker and our goal; slow them down instead of diving in | Buys time for teammates to come back |
| Play out from the back | 9+ | Keeper rolls to a defender who has moved wide; a midfielder drops to help | Keeps the ball instead of kicking it away; builds calm under pressure |
| Win it back fast | 9+ | First player presses the ball; the next two cut off passes | The other team is often messy just after winning the ball |
| Futsal pass and move | 9+ | Pass, then move into a new space straight away | Standing still makes you easy to mark |
| Overlap on the wing | 10+ | Winger holds the ball; a defender runs around the outside | Makes 2v1 against their full-back |
| Switch play | 10+ | Move the ball quickly from one side to the other | They crowd the ball side, so the far side has space |
| Pressure, cover, balance | 11+ | One presses, one covers behind, the far one tucks in | Nobody gets past easily and nobody is left free in the middle |
| Counter in three lanes | 11+ | After winning the ball, three players sprint forward in left, centre and right lanes | Their defenders cannot cover three lanes at once |
| Third-man run | 12+ | A passes to B, B lays it to C who is running into space | The defender watches the first pass and misses the runner |

### 6.3 Set pieces

Each shows where every player stands, with a kids' "why". Formats with no offside or with a build-out line (if the league uses one) get their own versions.

- **Attacking corner**: one near post, one far post, one on the edge of the box (catches clearances and stops counters), two at the back.
- **Defending corner**: players mark space in the six-yard area; one player stays upfield for the counter. Keep it the same every time so kids remember.
- **Free kick wall**: wall size by distance (1 to 3 players for young teams); everyone else marks a space. Skip walls where the league has a retreat rule instead **[confirm]**.
- **Kick-off routine**: a short pass back, then a switch to the winger.
- **Goal kick** (with build-out line where used): defenders split wide; keeper rolls to a defender; one midfielder drops.
- **Throw-in pattern**: thrower aims at a teammate who checks away then comes short; throw-and-go.
- **Futsal kick-in**: one short option, one long option, kicker steps in after passing.

## 7. Development profile (rubric)

### 7.1 Levels in words

| Stored | Shown to kids and parents | Meaning for coaches |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Learning it** | Starting to try it; needs help and many goes |
| 2 | **Getting it** | Can do it in practice without pressure |
| 3 | **Got it** | Does it in games most of the time |
| 4 | **Can teach it** | Does it under pressure and helps teammates do it |

Ratings compare a child only with their own earlier ratings. The app shows "You then, you now", highlights the **fastest developing** skill, and never totals or averages across children.

### 7.2 Skills per band (about six to nine, across the four corners)

| Corner | U8 | U10 | U12 | U14 | U16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Technical | Keeps the ball close; Uses both feet; Stops the ball | First touch; Inside-foot passing; 1v1 moves | First touch away from pressure; Passing range and weight; Weaker foot; Finishing | Receiving under pressure; Short and long passing; Heading (14+, within the rules) | Same as U14 plus crossing and finishing under pressure |
| Tactical | Goes forward with the ball | Finds space to receive; Stays goal-side | Position in our shape; Pass, dribble or shoot decision | Playing in units (pressure and cover); Transitions | Pressing triggers; Game management |
| Physical | Agility and balance | Speed and agility | Speed, agility, balance | Stamina; Bodyweight strength | Speed and power; Stamina |
| Psychological and social | Tries again after mistakes; Takes turns and listens | Effort and attitude after mistakes; Talks to teammates | Focus and confidence; Teamwork and leadership | Resilience; Coachability; Leadership | Leadership; Self-review |

### 7.3 How the term review works
1. Two minutes per child, in a swipe screen. Coach rates only what they have seen this term ("Not seen" is allowed).
2. Pick one strength to celebrate and one thing to work on. The app suggests the highest and lowest, but the coach chooses.
3. Write one or two sentences for the family, starting with something positive.
4. Coach-only notes stay coach-only.
5. Publish to families when the whole team is done (so families receive reviews together).

### 7.4 Self-rating (11+)
The child rates the same skills with the same words. Where the two ratings differ by two levels or more, the app suggests a one-to-one chat: "You rated your first touch Got it; I see Getting it. Let's look at it together."

## 8. Psychology rules (built into features)

### 8.1 Mastery climate: TARGET mapped to features

| Dimension | What a good coach does | Where it lives in the app |
|---|---|---|
| **Task** | Varied, challenging tasks, adjustable for each child | STEP easier and harder on every drill; small-sided games |
| **Authority** | Kids get real choices | Kids vote on the free-game format; child chooses their own goal; team picks the monthly focus value |
| **Recognition** | Private, specific, about effort and progress | Value recognitions; "You then, you now"; coverage nudge so every child is recognised |
| **Grouping** | Mixed, changing, cooperative groups | "Make groups" tool that mixes children randomly, never by rating |
| **Evaluation** | Self-referenced, effort and improvement, self-assessment | Words not numbers; fastest developing; self-rating 11+ |
| **Time** | Enough time to learn; flexible pace | Two-week curriculum blocks; run mode lets the coach extend a block |

### 8.2 Say this, not that (shown in tips and prompts)

| Say | Instead of |
|---|---|
| "Great run back to help, Ana!" | "Good job" (vague) |
| "What did you see there?" | "Why did you do that?" |
| "Mistakes mean you're trying. Next play!" | Shouting, or silence |
| "Your first touch is better than last month." | "You're better than Ben." |
| "Try our 3 keys and play with your heart." | "We have to win today." |
| "Who can show me a different way?" | "No, do it like this." |

### 8.3 Half-time and after the match
- Half-time prompt: **"Ask them first: what is working?"** then "What is hard?", then the coach adds one thing going well and one thing to change.
- After the match: "What was fun?" "What did you try?" "Who helped you?" Then the Celebrate slide, win or lose.
- **Mistake reset**: a team signal after a mistake (for Rangers: tap the badge on your chest, "Play with your heart", next play). The app shows it on the Values slide.

### 8.4 Recognition and badges
- **Recognition categories are the programme's values**: Rangers (Play With Your Heart, Teamwork, Sportsmanship, Perseverance, Respect, Integrity, Discipline, Community); FC New Mabu (Mindset, Attitude, Belief, Unity); Gila (Grind, Improve, Learn, Achieve).
- **Coverage nudge**: coach-only list of children with no recognition in six weeks.
- **Skill badges** (awarded when a coach sees it done in practice or a game): Sole Roll, Toe Taps 50, Cruyff Turn, Drag-back, Inside Hook, Outside Hook, Step-over, Scissors, Body Feint, La Croqueta, Wall Pass, First Touch Away, Weaker Foot Pass, Laces Strike, Placed Finish, Jockey Master, Keeper Ready.
- **Value badges**: one per value, earned through recognitions.
- **Never**: badges for goals, wins or "best player"; badges promised in advance as a reward ("do this and you get a badge"); public counts of badges per child.

### 8.5 For parents (shown in the parent app, one tip a week)
- Cheer effort, for every child, not only your own.
- Leave instructions to the coaches; mixed messages confuse kids.
- After the match, start with "I love to watch you play" and "What was fun?" before anything else.
- Never compare your child's development with another child's.
- Growth spurts are normal; late developers often catch up and pass early ones.

## 9. Safety rules

These are the club's defaults. The head coach can tighten them; loosening any of them needs the head coach and admin together. The app shows this wording exactly.

### 9.1 Heat (PAGASA heat index categories)

The app calculates the heat index for each hour of an outdoor event from Open-Meteo temperature and humidity, and uses the **highest** hour.

| Heat index | Category | Club actions (defaults, head coach approves) |
|---|---|---|
| Below 27°C | Normal | Water at every natural break |
| 27 to 32°C | Caution | Water break every 20 minutes; rest in the shade |
| 33 to 41°C | Extreme caution | Water break every 15 minutes; shade; shorter intense blocks; more rolling subs in matches; light-coloured shirts; avoid 10:00 to 16:00 for training where possible |
| 42 to 51°C | Danger | Train before 9:00 or after 16:00; 45 to 60 minutes maximum; cooling break every 10 to 15 minutes; no running fitness; children who are unwell or have asthma rest; ask organisers for cooling breaks or a later kick-off |
| 52°C and above | Extreme danger | Cancel or postpone outdoor training and matches |

**Heat illness: what to do** (shown in run mode and the match-day checklist): stop the child, move to shade, cool with water on skin, small sips of water. **Call for medical help immediately** if the child is confused, collapses, vomits, has hot skin, or stops sweating: this can be heat stroke.

### 9.2 Lightning
- If thunderstorms are forecast, the event card shows the shelter plan for the venue (a solid building or a closed vehicle; never under trees, goals, or open sheds).
- **When thunder roars, go indoors.** Resume only 30 minutes after the last thunder. The app's "Thunder heard" button runs the 30-minute countdown and restarts it with each tap.

### 9.3 Concussion (based on the Concussion Recognition Tool 6)

**The Builder must copy the observable signs, symptoms and red flags word for word from the official CRT6 document and link to it. The lists below are a summary for planning only.**

1. **Recognise**: signs such as lying motionless, slow to get up, unsteady on feet, blank or vacant look, confusion, facial injury. Symptoms such as headache, dizziness, blurred vision, nausea, feeling slowed down or "not right", sensitivity to light or noise.
2. **Red flags, call for medical help now**: neck pain, seizure, double vision or loss of vision, loss of consciousness, increasing confusion, weakness or tingling in arms or legs, repeated vomiting, severe or increasing headache, increasingly restless or agitated, deformed skull.
3. **Remove**: any sign or symptom means the child stops for the day. **If in doubt, sit them out.** Never leave the child alone; hand over to a parent or guardian.
4. **Record and block**: the app records the incident, notifies the family, and blocks the child from lineups.
5. **Return**: rest, then return to school first, then gradual activity. The child returns to contact training only with medical clearance, and **not to a competitive match within 21 days** of the injury (club default, from UK grassroots guidance) **[confirm]**. A head coach records the clearance date and who gave it.

### 9.4 Heading
| Ages | Default rule **[confirm]** |
|---|---|
| U11 and below | No heading in training. In matches, deliberate heading is discouraged and not coached. |
| U12 to U13 | Heading practice is low priority: at most one session a week, at most 5 headers each, with a light ball |
| U14 to U16 | At most one session a week, at most 10 headers each |

Drills containing heading are hidden or capped automatically by age. Based on The FA's approach; there is no PFF rule we found.

### 9.5 Growth and maturation
- Measure standing height every three months, at the club, shoes off, same wall tape, two measurements averaged.
- If growth is **7.2 cm a year or faster** (from two measurements at least 8 weeks apart), flag **Growth spurt** for coaches and family:
  - fewer jumps, landings and flat-out sprints for a few weeks; more technical work;
  - watch for knee pain below the kneecap or heel pain; do not play through pain; tell the family;
  - remind the child that clumsiness during a growth spurt is normal and temporary.
- Coaches see each child's birth quarter on the minutes report, as a reminder that late-born and late-maturing children need equal chances.

### 9.6 Wellness check (13+, optional)
Sleep, soreness and mood on a 1 to 5 scale before training. Use it to start a conversation ("You look tired, everything OK?"), never to select or drop a player.

### 9.7 Match-day and training checklist
- Water for every child (and a refill plan), shade, ice, first-aid kit, emergency contacts available offline.
- Goals anchored or weighted (portable goals can tip over and seriously injure children).
- Field walked for glass, stones and holes before play.
- Nearest hospital and directions known for every venue.
- At least two adults present.

### 9.8 Safeguarding basics
- Two adults with children; no adult alone with one child out of sight.
- No private messaging between adults and children; all messages go to parents.
- Photos and videos only of children with consent; never in changing areas.
- Children travel to away matches with their own family or with written permission.
- Concerns about a child's welfare go to the head coach straight away. The Philippines protects children under RA 7610; the club should name one welfare contact **[confirm]**.
